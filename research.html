Real-World Forecasting & Counterfactual Post-Training

DeepImagine · CT Open (COLM 2026)

  • Matches GPT-5 with Gemma-3-12B
  • Live, contamination-resistant benchmark
  • DeepImagine reframes clinical-trial outcome forecasting as stepwise counterfactual imagination. It models an intractable joint prediction as a Markov chain of single-factor perturbations and trains specialized Gemma-3-12B operators on natural and synthetic counterfactuals via SFT, RFT, and GRPO. Under a calibration assumption, multi-anchor aggregation provably converges to the true conditional, and we observe the predicted variance-reduction signature empirically. The model matches GPT-5 and outperforms RAG and traditional baselines.
  • CT Open is the first live, contamination-resistant benchmark for prospective clinical-trial forecasting. An agentic pipeline (search, scraping, multi-round verification) collects trials with time-stamped cutoffs, validated by external-database checks and a physician-scientist audit. The prediction challenge is hosted at ct-open.net.

Reinforcement Learning & Self-Improving Curricula

Question Begets Question

  • 5.6% → 16.5% pass@1
  • No saturation after 20 rounds
  • A self-evolving RL curriculum that teaches a model a skill it hasn't mastered, with no ground-truth reasoning traces: each round the model self-evaluates, synthesizes new problems, and fine-tunes with GRPO.
  • Under fixed data and compute, this breaks through an apparent RL plateau. The mechanism: seeding only from "mostly-solved" problems works, while harder or mixed seeds stay at baseline. Boundary-level seeds keep GRPO groups in a high-variance regime, whereas too-hard seeds zero out the group-normalized advantage. Gains transfer to unseen hard problems.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation & Long-Context Modeling

Single-Pass Document Scanning (COLM 2025, Oral Spotlight) · IR2 (LREC-COLING 2024, Oral) · BIRCO

  • 130M model beats 7B embedders
  • ~70× fewer TFLOPs
  • Generalizes past 256K tokens
  • Single-Pass Document Scanning frames long-document QA as RAG, where a cheap scanner retrieves the key sentences for an LLM generator. We turn a pretrained Mamba-2 into a single-pass scanner by replacing the LM head with a binary classification head that scores every sentence in one linear-time pass. This discriminative design beats generative fine-tuning of the same backbone by +26.5 points (33.5 → 60.0). Trained on 1M carefully designed synthetic examples, a 130M scanner beats 7B embedding models (NV-Embed-v2, GritLM).
  • IR2 is a synthetic query-generation method with controllable complexity that beats Promptagator across four contrastively fine-tuned architectures (E5-v2, SimCSE, RoBERTa, SPECTER-v2).
  • BIRCO is the first instruction-following information retrieval benchmark for complex user queries.

Trustworthy Evaluation at Scale

DORIS-MAE (NeurIPS 2023) · RoBBR (EMNLP 2025) · EvidenceBench (COLM 2025)

  • 165,000+ annotations in ~24 hours
  • 500× faster, 1,000× cheaper
  • DORIS-MAE uses statistically validated LLM-as-expert-annotator pipelines (pre-specified hypothesis tests, hierarchical bootstrap) that match expert panels of PhDs and MDs. It annotated 165,000+ document–aspect pairs in about 24 hours for under $150 with no loss in quality.
  • RoBBR splits risk-of-bias assessment into separate retrieval and reasoning subtasks. A two-stage ablation that swaps each component independently shows retrieval is the bottleneck, and that GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet have distinct strengths (retrieval vs. reasoning).
  • EvidenceBench is a benchmark for extracting evidence from biomedical papers.
